# Status, 2026-10-23 (update to 2026-10-19)

This is the newest tested work on `master` of the three forks (`MAXZVER/reims-vgpu`,
`MAXZVER/qemu-reims-vgpu`, `MAXZVER/metal2vulkan`). `vendor/qemu` points at `master` of the
QEMU fork; `Cargo.toml` still patches `metal2vulkan` to a sibling checkout
(`../metal2vulkan`, `master` of the fork, unchanged since the 2026-10-19 report). Earlier
reports stay in the tree; where they differ, this one wins.

Same lab as before: Intel i7-14700K, RTX 4060, Windows 11 with WHPX, macOS Tahoe at
5120x2160 and 240 Hz, 24 vCPUs. Queue numbers are our internal runs; each queue alternates
its arms, and only arms inside one queue are compared. Scene numbers are taken from the
action windows of a scripted heavy scene, not from raw slices of a run. This tree, built as
the lab's 2026-10-23 binary, was also run through a smoke test of 29 macOS apps (each
launched, a frame captured): all alive, no crash reports.

## Changed since 2026-10-19

### Performance (on by default unless noted; `=off` on the named switch restores the old path)

- **Apple Maps drag, about +45 %**: 34-41 fps on the 2026-10-22 build, 53.7 / 54.7 / 56.3
  fps on this one. The main steps:
  - `SUB_VHEAD` and `REC_VHEAD`: a merged sub-draw's stage-in window, and a record's, no
    longer copy the head of a vertex buffer that no draw of the record fetches (Core
    Animation's folded quads sit one after another in one buffer, so the k-th window held
    every quad before it). 93-95 % of those window bytes were such heads; the recording
    lane's copies fell from about 4.2 to about 1.5 GB/s. Only for buffers whose attributes
    are all per-vertex and that no shader reads as storage.
  - `SUB_VSTAGE_ZC`: a merged sub-draw's stage-in loads as guest runs, without a heap copy
    on the drain followed by a second copy into staging.
  - `LANE_BYTES`: recorder byte copies (merged sub-draw stage-in, storage bytes) moved to
    the recording lane.
  - `SUB_VMAX_SHARED`: a sub-draw that reads the record's index buffer is bounded by its
    own index range instead of the whole record's vertex bound.
  - `REAP`: a deferred draw's request (and the stage-in bytes it owns) is freed on a
    separate thread instead of on the recorder, which the drain waits for.
  - `MERGE_HEAVY` now defaults to 0: sub-draws with their own device buffers are not folded
    into a merged record (Maps 34.7 / 36.2 fps against 31.6 / 31.4 with the old cap of 8).
  - A merged sub-draw's guest vertex binds go through the import, not a heap copy, and a
    sub-draw's stage-in buffer keeps the content the prepare worker already read.
- **WebGL (heavy 5K canvas), about +13 %**: from about 48 to 52-55 fps, some launches at
  the 60 fps page cap. `GW_TOKEN_YOUNG_MS` (default 40): no guest-write token for a large
  surface incarnation younger than 40 ms. Safari re-backs its drawing surface on fresh pages
  every frame, and each token died before it could answer (about 19 % of the drain). The
  dirty tracker also skips sorting a page list that is already ascending.
- **Canvas 2D**: 61-68 fps, at Safari's own per-launch page cap.
- **`LAZY_SYNC`** (`REIMS_VGPU_LAB_LAZY_SYNC_PX=off` disables; threshold 4 000 000 pixels):
  a synchronize of a big never-presented surface completes before its frame lands in the
  guest's pages. The device takes those pages away from the guest with a fence while a
  landing thread copies the frame in, and a vCPU that touches them waits. In its A/B,
  canvas 61 to 71 fps and Maps 30 to 40-45, no artifacts. Fences survive QEMU's RAM
  remaps; the readback is submitted instead of waited on the drain; the copy is shared out
  over threads. Host ABI version 29.
- **Content-keyed resident copies (`CONTENT_RESIDENT`)**: implemented, opt-in and off. 82 %
  of Maps' stage-in bytes would hit, but hashing them costs as much as the copy it saves
  (Maps -10 %).

### New device: hardware video decode (opt-in)

- **`apple-vtpv-pci`** (`vendor/qemu`, PCI 106b:1a03): a paravirtual VideoToolbox device.
  The x86 Tahoe guest ships the VideoToolbox paravirtualization user framework but no
  kernel driver for it; this is the host side of our own replacement kext. Streams are
  decoded on the host (H.264, HEVC, VP9 and AV1 through libavcodec parsing and the D3D12
  Video hardware path, `hwbackend=dx`; H.264 and HEVC also through a Vulkan Video path)
  and the pictures are written into the guest's IOSurfaces. One decoder state per
  guest session, paced buffer requests, a decode thread, IODataQueue-exact replies. The
  device exists only with `-device apple-vtpv-pci` and needs the guest kext; it is not part
  of the default VM.
- Build note: `hw/misc/meson.build` links a minimal static ffmpeg (libavcodec and libavutil
  with only those four decoders and their D3D12/D3D11 hwaccels) from a sibling directory,
  `ffmpeg-min`, next to the checkout, and the Windows video libraries. Its build script is
  not published yet, so a QEMU build from a bare checkout needs that directory first. The
  Khronos Vulkan headers it uses are vendored under `hw/misc/vk/include`.

### Scaled present (opt-in lab switches, none on by default)

- NVIDIA RTX Video Super Resolution (`REIMS_VGPU_LAB_PRESENT_VSR`, with settle and tile
  variants that refine only what stands still), DLSS Super Resolution (`PRESENT_DLSS_SR`),
  DLSS Frame Generation and Reflex through Streamline (`PRESENT_DLSSG`), an optical-flow
  frame generator on `VK_NV_optical_flow` (`PRESENT_FG`), a supersampled present
  (`PRESENT_SS`) and a RAVU-Zoom upscale (`PRESENT_RAVU`, 3.5-6.8 ms a 5K present, not used
  by the launchers). The NVIDIA SDKs are not in this repository: `tools/rtx-vsr-shim`
  holds small C++ shims and benchmarks that are built against them separately. Streamline
  cannot restart in-process after a device loss; the present shuts it down with the lost
  device and recovers on plain Vulkan.

### Fixes

- A broad review pass over the translator and runtime (trace, taint and symbolic reviews),
  dozens of small fixes: translator panics contained and reported instead of killing the
  window thread; guest-sized buffers (frame scratch, linear sampled memo, fills, shifted
  vertex buffers) reserved fallibly; module id bounds and workgroup limits refused by name;
  vertex descriptor layout count bounded; stale registry notes, residency mirrors and
  write classes dropped with their object; a rgba32-float clear lands its own texels;
  storage incarnations wrap past zero; held mouse buttons released on focus loss; device
  destroy releases what a reset would.
- `ENCODER_MERGE` (a render encoder cut into one-packet command buffers run as one pass)
  was tried and reverted.

### Diagnostics

- `REIMS_VGPU_LAB_PRESENT_GPU_TIME` (GPU time per window present), a flush trace with the
  gap from a mapping's last published content, "why did this synchronize go lazy" counters,
  wait-by-site books for `LAZY_SYNC`, census counters for the head-skipping windows, and
  the `reims-fence` line with fence close/open wall time and page runs.
- Benchmarks under `tools/rtx-vsr-shim` (`vsrbench`, `frucbench`, `ofbench`, `dlssgcheck`,
  `dlssgwin`, `vkext`) for the NVIDIA paths above.

## Known open problems

- Chrome with Skia Graphite (Dawn on Metal): toolbar and page icons do not draw, and text is
  sometimes drawn over older text; both go away with Graphite off. Not yet explained.
- Two crashes inside the NVIDIA Vulkan driver with a 6912x2916 HiDPI backing; not reproduced
  yet (the crash stacks added on 2026-10-19 are meant to catch it).
- Safari video stops when switching Spaces (Chrome does not).
- 5K throughput is bound by guest-page dirty tracking and by the guest WindowServer's
  single-threaded Core Animation CPU on heavy pages.
- 5K WebGL frames are read back over PCIe (about 3.2 ms a frame on a PCIe x8 RTX 4060) and
  copied into the guest's pages before Safari's frame completes.
- Maps and WebGL are now partly bound by the guest's own CPU work, not only by the host.
